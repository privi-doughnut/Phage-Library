How to use this library

A catalog of bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria) and the exact spot on the bacterium each one grabs onto. No training needed — here's the whole thing in a minute.

1

Pick which data you're looking at

The three buttons at the top: Verified is the confident data (start here). Needs revision is data a scientist should double-check. All shows everything.

2

The list starts in closed categories

Rows are grouped into collapsible headers (by receptor type to start). Click any header to open it. Expand all flattens everything into one plain list; the same button then reads Collapse all to bring the categories back.

3

Regroup however you think

The Group menu reorganizes the whole list by receptor type, host bacterium, family, or host range. Choose No grouping for one flat list.

4

Search or filter to narrow down

Type any phage, bacterium, receptor, gene, or ID in the search box. The dropdown filters narrow the list further. Whatever you've applied shows as removable tags — click the × on a tag to undo it.

5

Jump in from the Overview

The tiles at the top show the shape of the library. Click any bar or number to jump straight to those phages.

6

Click a row for the full record

Any row opens a panel with everything: the receptor, its location on the cell, the source-database links, the paper it came from, and — for flagged rows — exactly why it needs a second look.

7

Take the data with you

Export view (CSV) downloads whatever you're currently looking at as a spreadsheet.

8

Sign in to edit

Lab members can sign in (top right, one-time email link) to edit any record, add new phages, mark rows Verified, or delete a genuinely wrong entry. Signed-out visitors keep full read/search/export access — signing in only unlocks writing.

Plain-language glossary

Phage
A virus that infects bacteria. Each targets specific bacteria.
Host receptor
The molecule on the bacterium's surface the phage latches onto to start an infection.
RBP
Receptor-binding protein — the part of the phage that does the latching.
GenBank link
Opens the DNA record for the receptor's gene at the NCBI database.
UniProt link
Opens the protein record for the phage's binding protein.
Receptor domain
The category of surface molecule (sugar coat, membrane protein, tail, etc.), shown by color.
Host range
Narrow = infects few bacterial strains; Broad = infects many. pred means predicted from the receptor, not measured in a lab.
Needs revision
The automated checks weren't fully confident, so a person should verify it.

Admin console

Manage lab admins, invites, and banned emails. This page is only visible to admins.

1

What admins can do that other signed-in users can't

Every signed-in lab member can already edit records, add new phages, toggle Verified/Needs revision, and flag a record for deletion. Admins additionally: permanently delete a record once its 72-hour flag cooldown has passed, grant or remove admin access for other people, and ban an email from writing anything.

2

Inviting a new admin

Type their email under "Invite as admin" below — no email is sent by this app. They become an admin automatically the next time they sign in normally (email link or password). Until then they show up under "Pending invites."

3

Removing an admin

Click "Remove" next to their email. You can't remove the last remaining admin — the app blocks it so nobody gets locked out of admin tools entirely.

4

The delete safeguard

Nobody can delete a record outright. Any signed-in user flags it for deletion from the record's detail drawer; from that moment it's on a 72-hour cooldown, visible to everyone via the small "flagged for deletion" tag on its row, and to admins specifically via the Pending deletion filter dropdown next to the other table filters. Anyone can cancel the flag before the cooldown ends. Only once the 72 hours have passed can an admin open that record's drawer and click "Delete now" — this is enforced by the database itself, not just hidden in the interface.

5

Banning an email

Type the email and (optionally) a reason under "Ban email" below. This blocks every write from that account — editing, adding, flagging, everything — enforced by the database, not just the interface. It does not force them out of an already-open session or block them from signing in again; they simply keep read-only access from then on. Unban at any time from this same list.
